# Part D Presentation Plan (2–3 Minutes)

> **Selected Figures:** Fig 2 (RQ1) + Fig 3 (RQ2) + Fig 4 (RQ3) — one figure per dimension
> **Core Message:** "Not all capital is created equal — the *pattern* of investment matters more than the *amount*."

---

## Why Three Figures?

The opening hook promises **three dimensions** (volume, milestones, velocity). Three figures pay off that promise one-for-one — the audience gets exactly what they were told to expect.

| Figure | Dimension | Chart Type | Presentation Strength |
|--------|-----------|------------|----------------------|
| **Fig 2** | Volume | Stacked Bar | Bars rising left→right = instant readability; IPO vs Acquired split adds analytical twist |
| **Fig 3** | Milestones | Bubble Chart | Red→green colour shift + threshold line = the most dramatic single moment |
| **Fig 4** | Velocity | Heatmap | Ties everything together; you only need to point at 2 cells, not the whole matrix |

**Narrative Arc:** "How much?" → "How many rounds?" → "How fast?" → Conclusion

**Why not Fig 1?** The TreeMap is an EDA overview — great for Reflection, but it doesn't answer a research question. In a 2.5-min slot, every second should advance the argument.

### Slide 2: Fig 2 — "Exit success rises with funding, but the exit pathway shifts" (0:20 – 1:00)

#### Slide Copywriting

```
Exit success rises with funding, but the exit pathway shifts
Dimension 1: Volume — How Much Money?

[Fig 2 inserted here]

• Exit rate climbs from 3% (<$1M) → 42% (>$500M)
• But the exit TYPE shifts:
  Low funding → acquired (bought by a larger company)
  High funding → IPO (listed on stock exchange)
• Baseline: 10.1% overall  |  n = 27,874 companies
```

> **Copywriting 注释：**
> - *Exit rate*（退出率）= 成功 IPO 或被 Acquired 的公司占比（相对于仍在运营或已关闭的公司）。可以理解为创业公司的「毕业率」。
> - *Acquired*（被收购）= 另一家公司直接买下这家创业公司，创始人和投资人拿到回报。例：Facebook 收购 Instagram。
> - *IPO* = Initial Public Offering（首次公开募股）——公司在证券交易所（如 NYSE、NASDAQ）上市，首次向公众出售股份。这是「大毕业」——通常只有规模较大、较成熟的公司才能做到。
> - *Baseline 10.1%* = 在数据集全部 27,874 家公司中，只有约十分之一成功退出。这是每根柱子的对比基准线。
> - *Funding brackets*（融资区间）= 按公司一生中融到的总金额分成六档，从「<$1M」到「>$500M」。

#### What You Say

> "Starting with volume. The X-axis is total funding in six brackets; Y-axis is exit rate *(see note 1)*. The bars climb steadily — from just **3%** for companies raising under a million, up to **42%** at the top."
>
> "But here's the interesting part — look at the colour. In the lower brackets, nearly all exits are **acquisitions** *(note 2)*, the blue portion. In the highest bracket, **IPO alone is 37%** *(note 3)*. So more money doesn't just increase your odds — it changes *how* you exit."
>
> "The dotted line is the 10.1% baseline *(note 4)*. You need at least 10 million to consistently beat it."

> **脚本注释（帮你理解，不要念出来）：**
>
> **(1) Exit rate**（退出率）= 每组创业公司中「成功毕业」的比例——要么被大公司买走（Acquired），要么上市（IPO）。反面就是一直没退出或者倒闭了。
>
> **(2) 低融资区间以 Acquired 为主**，因为：融资 <$10M 的小公司通常太小，达不到 IPO 的门槛（交易所对营收/规模有最低要求）。所以它们最好的「赢法」是被大公司买走——比如 Google 收购一个 5 人 AI 团队来获取技术。
>
> **(3) 高融资区间以 IPO 为主**，因为：融了 >$500M 的公司通常足够大（营收和估值都够），可以走上市路线。在这个体量下，IPO 的回报通常比被收购更高。比如 Uber、Airbnb——上市前融了几十亿美元。
>
> **(4) Baseline = 10.1%** 意味着数据集里所有公司中，只有约十分之一成功退出。这在 VC 领域其实很正常——大多数创业公司会失败。如果有人问「10% 是不是太低了？」，答案是：不低，这是 VC 的常态。
>
> **通俗表达小贴士：** 如果观众对 "exit" 一词看起来困惑，你可以自然地加一句："exit 就是投资人终于拿回钱的时刻——要么通过 IPO 上市，要么被另一家公司收购。"

#### Slide Notes (paste into PowerPoint/Google Slides notes panel)

> This stacked bar chart shows exit success rate (IPO + Acquired) across six funding brackets, with a sample size line on the secondary axis. Exit rate rises monotonically from 3% (<$1M) to 42% (>$500M). Notably, the composition shifts: acquisitions dominate lower brackets while IPO dominates the highest bracket. The dotted baseline at 10.1% represents the overall average across 27,874 companies.

*(54 words)*

#### Tips
- Point at the lowest bar, then the highest — the visual contrast does the work
- The "type of exit shifts" observation is your analytical depth moment — don't skip it
- If asked "why doesn't more money guarantee success?" → answer: correlation, not causation — successful companies attract more money, not the other way around

### Slide 3: Fig 3 — "A threshold emerges at Round 10 where exit rates surge" (1:00 – 1:45)

#### Slide Copywriting

```
A threshold emerges at Round 10 where exit rates surge
Dimension 2: Milestones — How Many Rounds?

[Fig 3 inserted here]

• Rounds 1–5: exit rate flat at ~10–15%
• Round 10: exit rate JUMPS +14 percentage points (13% → 27%)
• Interpretation: reaching Round 10 is a selection signal —
  only strong companies survive long enough to get there.

Threshold: Round 10  |  Δ = +14.2 pp
```

> **Copywriting 注释：**
> - *Funding rounds*（融资轮次）= 创业公司每次从投资人那里拿钱叫一「轮」。顺序是：Seed → Series A → Series B → C → D… 每一轮本质上就是创业公司通过一次考试——投资人重新评估「这家公司还值得押注吗？」
> - *Exit rate 在 ~15% 持平* = 无论你融了 1 轮还是 5 轮，最终退出的概率差不多都是 ~15%。早期轮次之间差别不大。
> - *+14 percentage points*（百分点）= 不是 +14%。从 13% 涨到 27% 是涨了 14 个**百分点**（绝对变化），退出率差不多翻了一倍。我们说 "percentage points (pp)" 是为了精确。
> - *Selection signal*（筛选信号）= Round 10 并不会神奇地「导致」成功。而是只有本身就强/有前景的公司才能活到第 10 轮。类比：马拉松完赛者身体好——不是马拉松让他们变好，而是身体好的人才能跑完。

#### What You Say

> "Next, milestones. Each bubble represents companies grouped by round count *(note 5)* — size is sample size, colour is exit rate. Red is low, green is high."
>
> "From Round 1 to 5, exit rate hovers around **10 to 15%** — fairly flat. But look at this dashed line at **Round 10** *(note 6)*. Exit rate jumps **14 percentage points** *(note 7)* in one step. The colour flips from warm to green."
>
> "This doesn't mean Round 10 *causes* success — it's a **selection signal** *(note 8)*. Companies that survive to late rounds have already passed multiple gates of investor scrutiny."

> **脚本注释：**
>
> **(5) Funding rounds 通俗解释：** 把创业公司想象成学生。每轮融资就像升一级——Seed 是幼儿园，Series A 是小学，Series B 是中学……每次投资人决定这家公司能不能「升级」。有些公司融 2–3 轮就提前退出（被收购）；有些经历 10+ 轮、花十多年才 IPO。
>
> **(6) 为什么偏偏是 Round 10？** 我们用了 first difference（一阶差分）的统计方法——找退出率跳幅最大的那一步。是数据告诉我们是第 10 轮，不是我们事先选的。大多数公司根本到不了第 10 轮（所以气泡很小），这恰恰说明能到的都是强者。
>
> **(7) Percentage points vs percent 的区别：** 13% → 27% 涨了 14 个 *percentage points*（27 减 13）。如果说「涨了 14%」，那在技术上意味着 13% × 1.14 = 14.8%，含义完全不同。讲的时候说 "14 percentage points" 显得精确专业。
>
> **(8) Selection signal——Q&A 核心概念：** 如果考官问「这不就说明好公司融资轮数更多吗？」——回答：对，这正是重点。这叫 *selection effect*（筛选效应）或 *survivorship bias*（幸存者偏差）。我们观察到的是 correlation（相关性），不是 causation（因果）。Round 10 的价值是作为一个 *diagnostic marker*（诊断标记）——像体温计读数，不是治疗方案。
>
> **通俗表达小贴士：** 如果观众看起来非技术背景，你可以加一句："就像淘汰赛——你存活的轮次越多，说明你越强。Round 10 就是决赛圈。"

#### Slide Notes (paste into PowerPoint/Google Slides notes panel)

> This bubble chart maps exit rate against funding round count (1–10+). Bubble size encodes sample size; colour encodes exit rate (red = low, green = high). Exit rates plateau at ~15% through Rounds 3–5, then surge at Round 10 (+14.2 percentage points). This threshold likely reflects a selection effect rather than a causal relationship — only strong companies survive to late rounds.

*(55 words)*

#### Tips
- The threshold reveal is your dramatic peak — slow down slightly when you say "14 percentage points"
- "Selection signal, not a cause" shows statistical maturity in one sentence
- If asked "is this causation?" → answer: no, it's correlation driven by selection. Strong companies both survive more rounds AND exit successfully.

### Slide 4: Fig 4 — "Moderate funding pace predicts success regardless of amount raised" (1:45 – 2:15)

#### Slide Copywriting

```
Moderate funding pace predicts success regardless of amount raised
Dimension 3: Velocity — How Fast?

[Fig 4 inserted here]

• X-axis: avg time between funding rounds (5 speed bins)
• Y-axis: total funding bracket (6 tiers)
• Each cell: exit rate for that combination
• Sweet spot: 6–24 months between rounds → highest exit rates
  across ALL funding levels

⚡ <6 months = burning cash too fast
🐢 >48 months = losing momentum
✅ 6–24 months = optimal cadence
```

> **Copywriting 注释：**
> - *Avg time between rounds*（平均融资间隔）= 如果一家公司 6 年内融了 4 轮，平均间隔约 2 年（24 个月）。我们分成了 5 档：从「<6 个月」（非常快）到「>48 个月」（非常慢）。
> - *Heatmap*（热力图）= 用颜色编码的网格。每个格子显示某个特定组合的退出率（比如：总融资 $10–50M 且融资间隔 12–24 个月的公司）。颜色越绿 = 退出率越高。
> - *Burning cash*（烧钱）= 花投资人的钱的速度超过赚钱的速度。所有创业公司初期都这样，但如果 <6 个月就要再融一轮，可能说明钱烧得太快（对投资人来说是坏信号）。
> - *Losing momentum*（失去势头）= 如果一家公司两轮之间等了 >4 年，可能意味着它很难吸引新投资人，或者市场/技术已经变了。
> - *Cadence*（节奏）= 融资的韵律/步调。可以想象成心跳——太快是恐慌，太慢是快要停了，稳定才是健康。

#### What You Say

> "Finally, velocity — the average time between rounds *(note 9)*. This heatmap *(note 10)* crosses funding speed on the X-axis with funding amount on the Y-axis. Each cell is an exit rate."
>
> "You don't need to read every cell. Just look at these two columns — **6 to 12 months** and **12 to 24 months**. They are consistently the greenest across every row. Whether you raised one million or five hundred million, a **6-to-24-month cadence** *(note 11)* is the sweet spot."
>
> "Too fast and you might be burning cash without traction *(note 12)*. Too slow and the market may have moved on."

> **脚本注释：**
>
> **(9) Average time between rounds** 通俗理解：如果你在 2015、2017、2019 年各融了一轮，那平均间隔就是 2 年（24 个月）。这个指标衡量的是融资的「节奏」——投资人是很快就回来（公司增长快），还是很久才回来（公司可能在挣扎，或者非常省钱）？
>
> **(10) 怎么向非技术观众解释 Heatmap：** 你可以说「就像一个用颜色标注的乘法表——行是融了多少钱，列是融资多快，颜色告诉你退出率。越绿越好。」不要在口头上说 "pivot table" 或 "cross-tabulation" 这种术语。
>
> **(11) 为什么 6–24 个月最优：** 很可能反映了一个健康的商业节奏：融钱 → 花 6–24 个月做产品/获取用户 → 展示进展 → 再融下一轮。快于 6 个月 = 还没来得及出成绩（或者钱烧太快了）。慢于 24 个月 = 可能增长困难或吸引不到投资人。
>
> **(12) "Burning cash without traction"**——这句话里有两个 VC 术语：
> - *Burn rate*（烧钱速度）= 公司每月花多少钱（比如每月 $500K 用于工资、服务器、营销）
> - *Traction*（牵引力/业务势头）= 进展的硬证据——用户增长、收入、合作伙伴。投资人想看到 traction 才愿意写下一张支票。
> - 合起来的意思：「钱花得很快，但没有拿得出手的成果」
>
> **通俗表达小贴士：** 你可以自然地用 Goldilocks（金发女孩）的比喻："就像金发女孩——不能太烫，不能太冷。每 6 到 24 个月融一轮的公司，似乎找到了刚刚好的温度。"

#### Slide Notes (paste into PowerPoint/Google Slides notes panel)

> This heatmap cross-tabulates funding speed (avg months between rounds) against funding amount, with cell colour indicating exit rate. Companies maintaining a 6–24 month funding cadence consistently show the highest exit rates across all funding brackets. This suggests funding velocity has an independent association with exit success, not merely a by-product of total capital raised.

*(50 words)*

#### Tips
- Don't try to explain every cell — point at the 6–24 month columns and say "these two columns"
- The "you don't need to read every cell" line actually *helps* the audience relax and focus where you point
- If asked "why not just show a simple bar chart for speed?" → answer: the heatmap lets us see that the speed effect holds *regardless* of funding amount — it's not just that rich companies happen to be fast

### Slide 5: Conclusion — "Capital pattern matters more than capital amount" (2:15 – 2:30)

#### Slide Copywriting

```
Capital pattern matters more than capital amount

  💰 Volume:     More funding → higher exit rates (3% → 42%)
                  but exit type shifts from acquisition to IPO

  🔄 Milestones:  Round 10 = selection threshold (+14 pp jump)
                  surviving more rounds signals company quality

  ⏱ Velocity:    6–24 month cadence = sweet spot
                  holds across ALL funding levels

Takeaway:
It's not just HOW MUCH you raise — it's HOW you raise it.
```

> **Copywriting 注释：**
> - 三个要点分别对应 slides 2–4 的三个小节标题，给观众一个视觉上的「回顾」。
> - *3% → 42%* 和 *+14 pp* 是整场演讲中最容易记住的两个数字——在结尾重复一次可以加深印象。
> - 最后一行刻意写得简短有力。它重新定义了整个分析的框架：资本的 *pattern*（结构、节奏、里程碑）比单纯的金额更重要。

#### What You Say

> "So — three dimensions, one consistent story. **More funding helps, but it changes how you exit — from buyout to IPO.** *(note 13)* **Surviving to Round 10 is a quality signal, not a cause.** *(note 14)* **And a steady 6-to-24-month pace outperforms at every level.** *(note 15)*"
>
> "The takeaway: it's not just how much you raise — it's *how* you raise it."

> **脚本注释：**
>
> **(13)** 呼应 Fig 2。你在提醒观众「不同的门」这个洞察——小公司被收购，大公司 IPO。两种都算成功退出，但本质上是完全不同的结果。
>
> **(14)** 呼应 Fig 3。"signal" 这个词很关键——你不是在说 Round 10 导致了成功，而是说它「标示」了质量。就像大学文凭不能让你变聪明，但它说明你大概率是聪明的。
>
> **(15)** 呼应 Fig 4。"at every level" 是关键短语——它告诉观众融资速度不仅仅是「有钱公司碰巧快」的副产品。即使控制了融资总量，6–24 个月的 cadence 依然胜出。
>
> **如果 Q&A 被问到「那创始人应该怎么做？」：** 你可以说："我们的数据是 correlational（相关性），不是 prescriptive（指导性）。但如果非要总结：融够有竞争力的资金，争取走到 Series B 以上，两轮之间不要太急也不要太拖。Pattern 比任何单一数字都重要。"

#### Tips
- Deliver the last sentence slowly and clearly — it's your takeaway line
- Don't add "that's all" or "thank you" — let the insight be the ending; the next speaker or moderator will transition
- The three-line recap (one per dimension) should feel like a rhythmic list — pause briefly between each

## Dos and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Orient axes/encoding before describing patterns | Jump straight to the conclusion on a new figure |
| Cite specific numbers ("42%", "14 percentage points") | Say vague things like "it goes up a lot" |
| Say "look at" or "notice" to direct attention | Let the audience wander the figure on their own |
| For Fig 4, say "just look at these two columns" | Try to explain every cell of the heatmap |
| End on the insight, not on "thank you" | Apologise for limitations mid-talk (save for Q&A) |

## Whole-Presentation Conclusion (~1 minute)

> **Context:** This is a separate section you deliver AFTER your Part D recap slide. It wraps up the entire group presentation and addresses the module's core question: *"Is it easier to understand the research through visualisation?"*

---

### Conclusion Slide Copywriting

```
Conclusion: What Did Visualisation Make Possible?

The raw data:
  • 27,874 startup records × 40+ columns
  • World Bank indicators across 136 economies
  • Timestamps, funding amounts, investor names, exit statuses…

Without visualisation:
  → Weeks of manual filtering to spot any pattern
  → No way to compare 4 dimensions simultaneously
  → Impossible to communicate findings in 10 minutes

With visualisation:
  ✅ Part A: One regression + one heatmap revealed that income
     alone doesn't explain startup activity
  ✅ Part B: A Sankey diagram made capital flows across continents
     readable in seconds
  ✅ Part C: Survival curves exposed cyclical failure patterns
     hidden in raw timestamps
  ✅ Part D: Three charts distilled 27,874 rows into one sentence —
     "it's not just how much you raise, it's how you raise it"

Visualisation didn't just illustrate our findings —
it made them discoverable in the first place.
```

### What You Say

> "So across this presentation, our group explored the global startup ecosystem from four angles. We used regressions and heatmaps to understand where startups thrive, Sankey diagrams to trace how capital flows across continents, survival curves to reveal when startups succeed or fail, and multi-dimensional charts to show what funding patterns lead to successful exits."
>
> "Together, these four parts tell a connected story: startup success is not random — it's shaped by geography, capital structure, timing, and funding rhythm. And throughout this process, visualisation is what allowed us to uncover these patterns. The raw data alone couldn't have told us any of this — it was by building and comparing visualisations that we moved from thousands of rows to clear, actionable insights."

> **脚本注释：**
>
> **(16) 整体结构是「我们用了…来理解…得出了…」：** 四个 Part 都遵循同一个句式：used [图表类型] to understand/analyse [问题] — and concluded/found [结论]。这样观众听起来有节奏感，而且每句话既点了 visualisation 工具，又点了发现，自然地证明了 visualisation 的价值，不需要单独提一个反问。
>
> **(17) 开头直接接上文：** 用 "So across this presentation" 自然地从你的 Part D 总结过渡到全组总结，不会让人觉得突然换了话题。
>
> **(18) 最后一段的逻辑：** 先说「raw spreadsheets 里看不到这些 pattern」（事实陈述），再说「是 visualisation 的过程让它们被发现」（归因），最后给出结论「visualisation 不只是展示已知结果，而是帮我们发现了未知的东西」。整个论证是顺畅递进的，不是反问。
>
> **通俗表达小贴士：** 如果时间紧，四个 Part 可以压缩成两句："Across four analyses, we used regressions, Sankey diagrams, survival curves, and heatmaps to explore different facets of the startup ecosystem — and each visualisation revealed patterns that were invisible in the raw data."

### Tips
- This is the **only part of the presentation** where you speak on behalf of the whole group — use "we" and "our", not "I" and "my"
- Pause before the final sentence — it's the ending of the entire presentation
- Don't say "thank you" after this — let the last insight land, then the moderator will wrap up
- If running short on time, cut the per-part summary to just: "Each of us found patterns that would have been invisible in raw spreadsheets."
